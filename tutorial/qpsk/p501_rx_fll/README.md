# Chapter 4d, extension: the QPSK receiver with a band-edge FLL (overlay `p501_qpsk_trx_fll`)

Status: **done**: integer model, RTL bit-exact in simulation, own overlay built (timing met), tested over the air against the B210 / GNU Radio.

The chain of the GNU Radio flowgraph, now in the FPGA: **FLL band-edge -> polyphase matched filter and timing loop -> Costas loop**.
It is a *separate* project: `rtl/qpsk_rx.v` and the overlay `p501_qpsk_trx` are untouched.

```
raw ADC samples (RAM, adc_clk) -> derotator  y = x e^{j phi}, phi += fr   (7 of the 26 clock cycles per sample)
   -> derotated sample RAM (two copies) -> engine of qpsk_rx.v (polyphase matched filter, AGC, timing loop, Costas loop, PRBS checker) as before
   once per symbol: error = normalised power difference of the two 55-tap band-edge filters over the last 55 derotated samples (4 multipliers give both filters)
   fr += GI e, phi += GP e, taking effect two blocks later (a fixed latency, so that the model and the RTL agree to the bit)
```

New registers (after those of the receiver): `0x54 FLL_CTRL` (bit 0 loop enable, a rising edge of bit 1 loads the frequency from `FLL_F0`), `0x58 FLL_GI`, `0x5C FLL_GP`, `0x60 FLL_F0`,
`0x64 FLL_FREQ` (R, turns x 2^32 per sample), `0x68 FLL_ERR` (R), `0x6C FLL_NBLK` (R, blocks measured), `0x70 FLL_LATE` (R, must stay 0).
Gain: `GI = round(gi / 2 pi x 2^32)`, `gi` in rad per symbol per unit of the normalised error (`fll_gains()`): about 0.002 to acquire, 0.0003 to track.
Sign: the FLL derotates, so its frequency is *minus* the offset it removes; offset of the signal = Costas frequency - FLL frequency.

## Verification

1. **Model** (offline, real B210 recordings and synthetic offsets): FLL + Costas acquires in 0.4 to 0.6 ms for offsets of 2.5 to 80 kHz. The Costas loop alone (2 pi/400) needs 0.8 ms at 9.7 kHz, 3.2 ms at 40 kHz and 41.6 ms at 80 kHz
   (2 pi/100: 1.9 ms at 80 kHz). On the P501 path the FLL alone is biased by about -3.7 kHz (tilt of the RX passband in the band edges); FLL + Costas together give the right total.
2. **RTL vs integer model** (xsim, 60000 samples): derotated samples, all 14986 block errors and all eight engine columns: **0 mismatches** with the default gains, with narrow gains (GI 205070) and with GI 600000, GP 300000, F0 -5000000.
   `overruns = 0`, `fll late = 0`.
3. **Build** (`vivado -mode batch -source create_project.tcl -tclargs FFT=0 QPSK=1 QPSKRXFLL=1 RUN=bit`): WNS +0.417 ns, WHS +0.010 ns (the same as the receiver without the FLL; the only violation, -0.002 ns pulse width on the RGMII clock, is in every build of this design).
   15744 LUTs, 21633 registers, 8.5 BRAM tiles, 73 DSPs (the receiver without the FLL: 14097 LUTs, 6.5 BRAM, 53 DSPs: the FLL costs 20 DSPs, 2 BRAM, 1650 LUTs).
   Overlay files `build/overlay/p501_qpsk_trx_fll.{bit,hwh}` (after `tools/fix_hwh.py`), board folder `/home/xilinx/jupyter_notebooks/p501_qpsk_trx_fll/`.
4. **Hardware**, B210 at 915 MHz + offset, TX gain 80 dB, P501 RX gain index 70, DQPSK PRBS-15 at 960 ksym/s:

| B210 - P501 | `acquire_fll()` locks | total offset from the long run (FLL and Costas, 1 s means) | original `acquire()`, hint 0 | pull-in time FLL + Costas | pull-in time Costas only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +9.7 kHz | 4/4 trials (3 at the first try, 1 at the second) | +9.6 kHz (Costas alone: +9.68) | locks | 0.0 to 0.3 ms | 0.0 to 0.2 ms |
| +39.7 kHz | 3/3, 1 try | +39.6 kHz (Costas alone: +39.65) | locks | 0.1 to 0.9 ms | 0.3 to 0.6 ms |
| -30.4 kHz | 3/3, 1 try | -30.45 kHz (Costas alone: -30.4) | locks | 0.2 to 0.9 ms | 0.4 to 0.7 ms |
| +79.5 kHz | (pull-in test only) | +79.0 kHz (instant readings) | locks | 0.3 to 0.4 ms | 1.1 to 2.8 ms |

   Pull-in time = time of the last change of the mismatch counter after the start, polled without a pause (20 us resolution); the loops are wide (FLL gi 0.002, Costas 2 pi/100). Between 6 s runs
   after the lock: 0 new mismatches at +9.7 kHz (12.4 Mbit), 0 at +39.7 kHz (8.6 Mbit), 0 at -30.4 kHz (8.6 Mbit); `overruns` 0 and `fll late` 0 throughout; constellation spread 4.4 / 3.7 / 6.8 degrees.

What the hardware tests say, honestly:

* The FLL receiver **locks first time without any frequency hint** and reports the right total offset, as the specification asked.
* The original receiver also locks first time here, even at +-30 to 40 kHz with no hint: with a wide Costas loop and 80 ms of settling the pull-in works far beyond the nominal 960 kHz / N.
  So the FLL does not make a lock possible that was impossible; it makes it **faster at large offsets** (3 to 7 times at 80 kHz; no clear difference up to 40 kHz) and does not depend on the settling time of the Python procedure.
* The FLL is **noisy**: its frequency scatters by about +-0.5 to 1 kHz around the mean, even with the narrow gain (the band-edge power is data dependent), and the Costas loop follows with the opposite sign
  (totals are steady, the two parts are not). This is the known behaviour of this FLL type; the constellation spread is a little larger than without it at the large offsets (6.8 degrees at -30 kHz).
  The cure is a handover: after the lock, `rx.freeze_fll()` loads the FLL with its mean frequency and stops its loop. Measured with the notebook (experiment 2, 400 readings, 3.4 Mbit, weak signal, AGC gain about 9):
  running: FLL std 2.0 kHz, Costas std 3.9 kHz, total offset std 4.3 kHz, 26,411 bit errors; frozen: FLL std 0, Costas std 0.3 kHz, total std 0.3 kHz, 36 bit errors. In a cleaner moment (`dbg` runs, AGC gain about 7)
  the Costas scatter fell from about +-1 kHz to +-0.25 kHz and nothing was lost at the freeze, with the loop on or off.
* The FLL is sensitive to the signal level. The B210's output drifted during the tests (AGC gain 6.3 to 14); at the weaker end the FLL scatter grew to several kHz, and the pull-in of both receivers (FLL + Costas and Costas alone)
  sometimes took 100 to 300 ms or failed within the 0.3 s window (in one notebook run 5/6 and 2/6 locked, in another 6/6 and 6/6). The first test series above (clean signal) is the better case; compare the two before drawing conclusions about the speed.
* The pull-in numbers are from 3 to 4 trials per cell, enough to see the order of magnitude, not a statistic.

## The open-loop 4th-power estimator (not used)

The first idea (differential 4th-power estimator, `qpsk_fe_fixed.py`) was studied offline: with magnitude weighting the estimate is biased by -15 % on real data (AM/PM correlation of the pulse-shaped signal); with unit-magnitude symbols
(CORDIC angle x 4, a table) it is unbiased over +-115 kHz. It was dropped when the specification changed to the closed-loop chain of the GNU Radio flowgraph (FLL -> polyphase -> Costas); `fe_study.py` regenerates `tutorial/img/qpsk_fe_study.png`.
